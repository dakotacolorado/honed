# UX review

## Start from people and their goals
Before looking at details, name who the page is for and the few journeys that matter most to them: arriving for the first time, doing the core task, recovering from a mistake, coming back later. If you don't know, infer it from the product and say what you assumed.

## Walk each journey for real
Use the page the way a person would: click, type, submit, go back, refresh, narrow the window. At each step, notice what the person is trying to do, what they see, and where they would hesitate, guess, or get stuck. Visit the states people actually meet: empty, loading, error, success, and too much content.

## Judge with established principles
- **Clarity:** it is obvious what this is, what to do next, and what just happened.
- **Effort:** no step, decision, or bit of typing that could be removed or defaulted.
- **Feedback:** every action is acknowledged; errors say what went wrong and how to fix it, where it happened.
- **Consistency:** the same thing looks, reads, and behaves the same way, here and in the platform's conventions.
- **Forgiveness:** mistakes are easy to undo, and anything destructive asks first.
- **Accessibility:** usable by keyboard with visible focus, readable contrast, labeled controls, works zoomed and on a phone.
- **Trust:** honest wording, no surprises, nothing that pressures or tricks.

## Report
Open with your overall impression and the three issues that matter most. Then, for each finding: the journey and step, what happens, why it matters to the person (name the principle), how severe it is (blocker, major, minor, polish), and a concrete suggestion. Say what works well, so a fix doesn't lose it, and what you couldn't check.
